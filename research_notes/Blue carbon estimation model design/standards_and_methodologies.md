# How carbon standards require blue carbon credits to be quantified, monitored and verified

Scope: Verra VM0033 v2.1 (and the draft v3.0), the VCS Standard v5.0, the AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool v4.2, CDM AR-AM0014 and AR-TOOL14, India's CCTS adoption of AR-AM0014 (BEE BM FR05.001), the IPCC 2013 Wetlands Supplement Ch. 4, Plan Vivo, Gold Standard and ICVCM.
Research date: 2026-10-08. Primary PDFs were downloaded and read in full text where possible. Page numbers are PDF pages; for IPCC they match the printed "4.x" pages.

Note on reading the IPCC tables: footnote superscripts in the PDF text sit next to the numbers (e.g. "1.622" is −1.62 with footnote 2). The values below have been corrected for this using the 95% CIs.

## 1. Verra VM0033: carbon pools, soil carbon, uncertainty, buffer, baseline, CH4/N2O

### Takeaway
VM0033 v2.1 (active since 4 Sep 2023) is still the main voluntary-market blue carbon methodology. A major revision to v3.0 (modular, with a dynamic performance benchmark) finished public consultation on 5 Oct 2026 and has not yet been published.
- **Pools:** soil organic carbon (SOC) is the core pool. Tree biomass is mandatory in the baseline but optional (it can be conservatively omitted) in the project.
- **Soil carbon change:** can come from proxies, models, published data, default factors (1.46 t C/ha/yr for marsh and mangrove), historical or chronosequence data, or field measurement.
- **Uncertainty:** above 20% (90% CI) or 30% (95% CI), the excess is deducted.
- **Buffer:** set by the AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool (NPRT), with a minimum risk rating of 12.
- **Soil credit cap:** limited to the 100-year net benefit (NER_RWE-max).
- **Additionality:** demonstrated with an activity method (VMD0052).

### Cited findings

**Version and status**
- VM0033 v2.1 became active on 4 September 2023. The changes were minor: it requires the latest AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool (NPRT) for the risk of erosion or submergence from sea-level rise, and it lets the project area change to allow landward migration. Its mitigation-outcome label eligibility is listed as "Pending Methodology Update" — [Verra VM0033 v2.1 page](https://verra.org/methodologies/vm0033-methodology-for-tidal-wetland-and-seagrass-restoration-v2-1/)
- VM0033 applies globally to tidal forests (mangroves), tidal marshes and seagrass meadows. Removals are expected "primarily by increasing both biomass and autochthonous soil organic carbon" — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Verra replaced CDM AR-AM0014 and AR-AMS0003 with VM0033 v2.0 for new VCS projects. AR-AM0014 projects listed by 31 Aug 2022 had to validate and request registration by 28 Feb 2023 — [Verra announcement](https://verra.org/verra-replaces-cdm-ar-am0014-and-ar-ams0003-methodologies-with-vm0033-methodology-for-tidal-wetland-and-seagrass-restoration-v2-0/)

**Carbon pools (Table 1, p.21–22)**

| Pool | Status in VM0033 v2.1 |
|---|---|
| Above-ground tree biomass | Included. Mandatory in the baseline; in the project scenario it "may be included or conservatively omitted". |
| Above-ground non-tree biomass | Included |
| Below-ground biomass | Mandatory in the baseline; optional (conservative omission allowed) in the project |
| Litter | Optional, "only included indirectly in association with the quantification of herbal mass" |
| Dead wood | Optional |
| Soil | Included (the SOC stock "may increase due to the implementation of the project activity") |
| Wood products | Included |

- A pool or source may be treated as de minimis if the omitted items together are under 5% of the total GHG benefit. AR-Tool04 may be used to test this — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.21–22](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Herbaceous biomass default: 3 t C/ha for strata with 100% herbaceous cover, scaled 1:1 with vegetation cover below 100%. It may be claimed only for the first year of the crediting period, and not if AR-Tool14 is used. The figure comes from 1.3 kg d.m./m² × 0.45 (organic matter to carbon) × 0.5 (average of peak and minimum biomass) — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.30](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)

**How soil carbon change is estimated (Sections 8.1.4 and 8.2.4)**
- Allowed approaches, in this order:
  1. proxies
  2. models
  3. published data
  4. default factors
  5. field-collected data
  6. historical or chronosequence data

  "Default factors and emissions factors cannot be used in the presence of published data suitable for use in the project area" — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.32–33](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **Proxies** (such as water table depth or subsidence) must be "strongly correlated with the value of interest". They must be shown equivalent or better than direct measurement, and developed or tested in the same or similar region, geomorphology and hydrology. **Models** must be validated with direct measurements from systems with similar water table, salinity, tidal hydrology, sediment supply and plant community — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.32–33](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **Default SOC accumulation for tidal marsh and mangrove:** 1.46 t C/ha/yr × 44/12 (= 5.35 t CO2/ha/yr), from the median of the Chmura et al. 2003 synthesis (Eq. 33). It is scaled by vegetation cover:
  - cover of 50% or more: the full rate;
  - cover below 15%: zero;
  - cover from 15% to 50%: linear interpolation.

  When this default is used, an allochthonous-carbon deduction is mandatory. The default "may only be applied to non-seagrass tidal wetland" systems — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.36](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **Allochthonous carbon deduction** (Section 8.1.4.2.7 / 8.2.4.2.2): mandatory for the project scenario, unless the proponent shows the allochthonous carbon would not have been preserved elsewhere. It can be estimated from field cores (% allochthonous by %OM), from defaults, or from validated models. It is zero for organic soils, and for seagrass only under specific conditions — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- For seagrass soils, %C may be estimated from %OM:
  - %C = −0.21 + 0.40(%OM) when %OM < 20% (Eq. 97);
  - %C = −0.33 + 0.43(%OM) when %OM > 20% (Eq. 98).

  Accretion may be measured with feldspar marker horizons, among other methods — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Baseline soil cores "must be collected within 2 years prior to the project start" — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)

**Peat depletion time (PDT), soil organic carbon depletion time (SDT) and the 100-year cap**
- **PDT** = peat depth at t0 ÷ baseline peat-loss rate (Eq. 1). It limits how long baseline emission reductions from organic soils can be claimed. Projects that do not claim reductions of baseline soil emissions need not estimate it — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.12–13](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **SDT** for mineral soils is either 5 years by default, or baseline C stock ÷ C-loss rate. It is set to zero for sites drained more than 20 years before the start, or where significant erosion occurs. PDT must be re-determined every 10 years — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.13, p.25](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **Soil credit cap:** claims from the soil carbon pool are limited to one of:
  - the difference in remaining SOC between project and baseline at t = 100 years ("total stock approach");
  - the difference in cumulative SOC loss over 100 years ("stock loss approach").

  This must be calculated ex ante with conservative parameters — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.19](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Where sea-level rise may cause loss of wetland, "NER_RWE-max = NER_RWE at t = 100" (Eq. 86). This is the maximum claimable at any point in the crediting period — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.61](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Note: the brief's phrase "Soil Organic Carbon Maximum" does not appear in v2.1. The equivalent concepts are this 100-year cap (NER_RWE-max) and PDT/SDT.

**Uncertainty (Section 8.5, p.61–64)**
- The precision target is a confidence interval of 20% or less of the mean at 90% confidence, or 30% or less at 95% — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.61](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- "Where this precision level is met, no deduction must result for uncertainty. Where this precision level is exceeded, a deduction equal to the amount that the uncertainty exceeds the allowable level must be applied":
  - adjusted_NER_t = NER_t × (100% − NER_ERROR + allowable_uncert) (Eq. 92).
  - Total uncertainty combines baseline and project uncertainty, each expressed at a 90% CI.
  - Where an uncertainty "cannot be calculated", the proponent may use 0% for that component only if it justifies the number used as conservative.

  — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.61–64](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Sampling uses permanent plots where stock changes are monitored. AR-Tool03 may be used to size the sample. The CI must be 90% or 95%, and if its width exceeds 20% or 30% respectively, the deduction applies — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.107](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)

**Buffer and non-permanence**
- VCUs at t2 = (adjusted_NER_t2 − adjusted_NER_t1) less the buffer (Eq. 93; the operator renders oddly in the PDF text).
  - Buffer_t2 = (NER_stock,t2 − NER_stock,t1) × Buffer%_t2 (Eq. 94).
  - NER_stock excludes non-CO2 soil and burning emissions and fossil-fuel emissions, so the buffer applies only to the stock change.
  - Buffer% comes from the latest VCS AFOLU NPRT.

  — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.64](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **AFOLU NPRT v4.2:**
  - Overall risk = internal + external + natural risk.
  - "The minimum risk rating shall be 12, regardless of the risk rating calculated" (§2.5.2).
  - A rating above 60 fails the analysis (§2.5.3).
  - v4.x added projected climate change and sea-level-rise (SLR) risk.
  - A management-plan horizon of 5 years and a reassessment cadence are also referenced.

  — [AFOLU NPRT v4.2 PDF](https://verra.org/documents/afolu-non-permanence-risk-tool-v4-2); [Verra release note](https://verra.org/verra-releases-updated-afolu-non-permanence-risk-tool/)
- Note: the risk rating maps to the buffer percentage, so a minimum rating of 12 means at least 12% of stock-change credits withheld. This is an inference from the tool's structure, in which the overall risk rating is applied as the buffer percentage.

**Additionality and baseline**
- "This methodology uses an activity method for the demonstration of additionality of tidal wetlands conservation and restoration project activities… use Module VMD0052". Additionality elements of the CDM combined tool are disregarded — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.25–26](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- The baseline scenario must be reassessed every 10 years. Baseline emissions are GHG_BSL = GHG_BSL-biomass + GHG_BSL-soil + GHG_BSL-fuel (Eq. 18) — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- Baseline soil parameters may come from historical project-area data or "chronosequence data collected at similar sites" (Section 8.1.4.2.6). Tree baseline inventory may use "a proxy area in the same region" — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.37](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)

**CH4 and N2O defaults with salinity thresholds**
- **CH4:** where the salinity average or low point is above 18 ppt, the default is 0.011 t CH4/ha/yr × GWP (Eq. 60). Where it is 20 ppt or more, the default is 0.0056 t CH4/ha/yr (Eq. 61). Using 0.011 in the baseline and 0.0056 in the project to manufacture a reduction is prohibited — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.47](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **N2O defaults** (t N2O/ha/yr) apply unless there are direct N inputs such as wastewater or fertilised systems — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.49](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)

  | System | Salinity | Default (t N2O/ha/yr) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Open water | >18 ppt | 0.000157 |
  | Open water | >5 and ≤18 ppt | 0.00033 |
  | Open water | other | 0.00053 |
  | Non-seagrass wetland | >18 ppt | 0.000487 |
  | Non-seagrass wetland | >5 and ≤18 ppt | 0.000754 |
  | Non-seagrass wetland | other | 0.000864 |

**Draft v3.0**
- Consultation ran 3 Sep–5 Oct 2026 (development ID M0167, led by Silvestrum). It introduces:
  - a modular framework;
  - a new dynamic performance benchmark for afforestation, reforestation and revegetation plus restoration of wetland ecosystems (ARR+RWE) in tidal wetlands;
  - leakage handling drawn from VMD0054 v1.1;
  - updated default factors;
  - draft modules: VMD00BM (Biomass and Dead Wood), VMD00TW (Tidal Wetland Soils), VMD00ARR (Tidal Wetland ARR), VMD00LK (Leakage).

  — [Verra consultation page](https://verra.org/consultation-revision-to-tidal-wetland-and-seagrass-restoration-methodology-vm0033/)

### Inferences
- The project's "observed − baseline − uncertainty" structure is close to VM0033. Three differences matter:
  - VM0033 deducts only the uncertainty above 20% (90% CI), not the whole CI.
  - The buffer applies only to the stock-change component, after the uncertainty adjustment.
  - Credits are issued on the cumulative-difference basis (NER_t2 − NER_t1), which matches the "− already issued" term.
- A satellite-only student model is closest to the "proxy" route. VM0033 requires a proxy to be strongly correlated with, and validated against, field measurements in similar systems. Satellite-derived SOC change without cores would not meet v2.1.

### Gaps
- The v3.0 draft PDFs and module text were not read. Whether VMD00TW or the dynamic benchmark explicitly allows remote-sensing quantification of stocks is unconfirmed.
- The exact NPRT v4.2 sea-level-rise scoring table was not extracted.

## 2. Remote sensing and dynamic baselines in Verra (VM0047, VCS Standard v5.0)

### Takeaway
Verra accepts remote sensing for three things: area and stratification, monitoring loss events or permanence, and (in VM0047) a relative "stocking index" used for dynamic performance benchmarks against matched control areas. Absolute carbon stocks still have to be anchored in field plots. A dynamic benchmark is now proposed for tidal wetlands in VM0033 v3.0.

### Cited findings
- **VM0047 (ARR):** an area-based approach uses a stocking index (SI) derived from remote sensing as a relative measure of vegetation change. It compares the project with matched control areas that form the performance benchmark. Verra vetted Sylvera, Kanop and Chloris Geospatial as SI data providers. VM0047 v1.0 and v1.1 are CCP-approved by ICVCM — [Verra DSP announcement](https://verra.org/verra-announces-vetted-data-service-providers-for-arr-methodology/)
- A vendor (Sylvera) states that VM0047 requires control plots comparable in land cover, vegetation structure and trajectory. This is a vendor source — [Sylvera guide](https://www.sylvera.com/es/blog/geospatial-biomass-data-carbon-methodology-requirements)
- **VCS Standard v5.0** (16 Dec 2025): where an instance leaves a project, or monitoring cannot follow the methodology, Verra "may approve the use of a robust remote sensing-based approach… to detect loss events". Remote sensing may also be used in the post-crediting period for the rest of the project longevity (§3.2.19) — [VCS Standard v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0)
- VM0033 v2.1 allows remote sensing for:
  - delineating the project area: "Remotely sensed data, published topographic…" (p.~16);
  - measuring subsidence and burn-scar depth (Ballhorn et al. 2009);
  - extracted timber volume.

  Soil subsidence must be monitored at least once a year, with 10 or more poles per stratum. For inaccessible areas, vegetation cover may serve as an indicator — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF p.112](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **CDM AR-TOOL14 v04.2:** "Remote sensing data may also be used in conjunction with data from measurements conducted in sample plots." Double sampling regresses plot biomass on a cheap secondary variable, and it requires a linear relationship. Worked example: NDVI in 2,000 plots with tree measurement in 150 plots cut the variance of the mean by one third — [AR-TOOL14 v04.2](https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/ARmethodologies/tools/ar-am-tool-14-v4.2.pdf)
- **Gold Standard** Methodology for Sustainable Management of Mangroves (with FORLIANCE, 22 Aug 2024) allows remote sensing alongside ground data as an accepted quantification approach — [Gold Standard news](https://www.goldstandard.org/news/new-mangrove-methodology-featuring-remote-sensing-expands-nature-based-solutions-from-gold-standard)

### Inferences
- The donor-plot synthetic control is conceptually aligned with VM0047-style dynamic performance benchmarks, which use matched controls and relative remote-sensing indices.
- Sentinel-2 indices fit as the secondary variable in double sampling (AR-TOOL14), or as the SI in a benchmark. They would still need calibration against plot or allometric biomass.
- Sentinel-1/2 cannot observe SOC directly. Soil credits would rely on default rates (IPCC or VM0033 1.46 t C/ha/yr) gated by vegetation cover, which satellite data *can* measure. VM0033's cover-threshold rule (0% below 15% cover, linear to 100% at 50% cover) maps directly onto a satellite canopy-cover product.

### Gaps
- The exact VM0047 matching criteria (number of controls, buffer distance, pre-start matching window) were not read from the methodology text.
- The draft VM0033 v3.0 dynamic benchmark design is not yet public in detail.

## 3. CDM AR-AM0014, AR-TOOL14 uncertainty discounts, and India's CCTS adoption

### Takeaway
AR-AM0014 v3.0 (valid from 4 Oct 2013) credits mangrove A/R. Above- and below-ground biomass are mandatory, litter is excluded, and dead wood and SOC are optional. SOC can use a default of 0.5 t C/ha/yr for 20 years. India's CCTS offset mechanism adopted it verbatim as BEE BM FR05.001 v1.0 (27 Mar 2025), so this is the most India-relevant rulebook. AR-TOOL14 discounts estimates whose uncertainty (90% CI) exceeds 10%, using a stepped table.

### Cited findings
- **AR-AM0014 versions:** v3.0 has been active since 4 Oct 2013 (v2.0.0: 23 Nov 2012–3 Oct 2013; v1.0.0: 3 Jun 2011–22 Nov 2012). A draft v4.0 for standardized baselines exists (ARWG38 Annex 4) — [CDM AR-AM0014 v3 draft revision](https://cdm.unfccc.int/Panels/ar/038/meeting/ARWG38_Annex_04_AR-AM0014_ver03.0.pdf); [CDM methodology DB](https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/DB/KMH6O8T6RL3P5XKNBQE2N359QG7KOE/)
- **BEE BM FR05.001 v1.0** ("Afforestation and reforestation of degraded mangrove habitats", Offset Mechanism under Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, published 27 Mar 2025) "is adopted and refers to the latest approved version of… AR-AM0014 (as valid from 04 October 2013)" — [BEE BM FR05.001](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **Applicability:**
  - degraded mangrove habitat;
  - more than 90% of the area planted with mangrove species;
  - if more than 10% is planted with non-mangrove species, no alteration of hydrology;
  - soil disturbance on no more than 10% of the area.

  — [BEE BM FR05.001 §3.2](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **Pools (Table 1):**
  - above-ground biomass: yes;
  - below-ground biomass: yes;
  - litter: no ("high turnover and displacement due to tidal currents");
  - dead wood and SOC: optional.

  CH4 and N2O from biomass burning are included — [BEE BM FR05.001 §4.1](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **SOC (Eq. 4):** ΔSOC = 44/12 × Σ A_PLANT,t × dSOC_t × 1 yr.
  - Default dSOC = 0.50 t C/ha/yr from planting to planting + 20 years, then 0.
  - The default applies "unless transparent and verifiable information can be provided to justify a different value".

  — [BEE BM FR05.001 §4.5](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **Net removals:** ΔC_AR = ΔC_ACTUAL − ΔC_BSL − LK. Baseline = change in tree, shrub and dead-wood stock (via BM-T-AR-0004/0003). Additionality and baseline use BM-T-AR-0001 (the combined tool). Credits are temporary (tCCC) or long-term (lCCC) per verification period, and a negative lCCC means reversal credits must be replaced — [BEE BM FR05.001 §4.4–4.8](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **Monitoring:**
  - stratify if biomass is heterogeneous, with ex-post stratification revised after disturbances;
  - precision requirements as in BM-T-AR-004 (the AR-TOOL14 equivalent);
  - data archived for at least 2 years after the last crediting period.

  — [BEE BM FR05.001 §5](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)
- **AR-TOOL14 v04.2** (EB 85 Annex 22, 24 Jul 2015): uncertainty = standard error of the mean × 90% t-value ÷ mean, in %. If the uncertainty of the stock or stock change is above 10%, the estimate is made conservative using Appendix 2 — [AR-TOOL14 v04.2](https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/ARmethodologies/tools/ar-am-tool-14-v4.2.pdf)

  Appendix 2, Table 1 discount (as % of U):

  | Uncertainty U | Discount |
  |---|---|
  | U ≤ 10% | 0% |
  | 10 < U ≤ 15 | 25% |
  | 15 < U ≤ 20 | 50% |
  | 20 < U ≤ 30 | 75% |
  | U > 30 | 100% |

  Worked example: 60 ± 9 t d.m./ha (U = 15%) gives a discount of 2.25. That means 62.25 in the baseline and 57.75 in the project. Biomass estimation options:
  - sample-plot measurement (allometric equations or BEF);
  - modelling;
  - proportionate crown cover (baseline only, when crown cover is below 20% of the host-Party forest threshold);
  - double sampling with remote sensing.

  — [AR-TOOL14 v04.2](https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/ARmethodologies/tools/ar-am-tool-14-v4.2.pdf)
- Global Carbon Council GCC MA002 (mangrove forestry) is also based on AR-AM0014 v3.0 — [GCC MA002](https://www.globalcarboncouncil.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/GCC-MA002-Methodology-for-Forestry-Activities-on-Degraded-Mangrove-Habitats.pdf)

### Inferences
- For an Indian student project, approximating BM FR05.001 / AR-AM0014 is the most defensible choice: field allometry for above- and below-ground biomass, an optional SOC default of 0.5 t C/ha/yr (1.83 t CO2/ha/yr) for 20 years, and the AR-TOOL14 stepped discount. It is simpler than VM0033 and is legally recognised under CCTS.
- The AR-TOOL14 discount is applied to the mean, raising the baseline and lowering the project value. That differs from subtracting a full CI and is less punitive at low U.

### Gaps
- The BEE tool texts BM-T-AR-0001 to 0005 were not retrieved, so it is unconfirmed whether India changed any thresholds from the CDM tools.
- Verification frequency under CCTS was not found in BM FR05.001; it is in the CCTS "Detailed Procedure", which was not read.

## 4. IPCC 2013 Wetlands Supplement, Chapter 4 (Coastal Wetlands): Tier 1 defaults

### Takeaway
The commonly quoted figures are confirmed. Table 4.12 gives Tier 1 soil carbon accumulation on rewetting, revegetation or creation:

| Ecosystem | EF_REWET (t C/ha/yr) | 95% CI | t CO2/ha/yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mangrove | −1.62 | 1.3–2.0 | ≈5.94 |
| Tidal marsh | −0.91 | 0.7–1.1 | ≈3.34 |
| Seagrass | −0.43 | 0.2–0.7 | ≈1.58 |

Negative values mean removal. The rates apply from the initiation of vegetation re-establishment. Soil stocks are given to 1 m depth.

### Cited findings
All from the [IPCC 2013 Wetlands Supplement Ch. 4 PDF](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/wetlands/pdf/Wetlands_separate_files/WS_Chp4_Coastal_Wetlands.pdf).

**Mangrove biomass and dead organic matter (p.4.13–4.17)**

| Table | Parameter | Value |
|---|---|---|
| 4.2 | Carbon fraction of mangrove above-ground biomass | 45.1% (95% CI 42.9–47.1) |

### Inferences
- VM0033's own default (1.46 t C/ha/yr) is lower than the IPCC mangrove value (1.62) and is applied with cover scaling and an allochthonous deduction. A conservative student model should use 1.46 (or India's 0.5) rather than 1.62.
- To convert stock (t C/ha) to t CO2e, multiply by 44/12 ≈ 3.667. Keep stock (t C/ha) and flux (t C/ha/yr) separate, and convert to CO2e only at the end.
- Indian Sundarbans and Bhitarkanika are tropical, so Tropical Wet values (192 t d.m./ha, R = 0.49) are the appropriate Tier 1 benchmarks for checking satellite-derived above-ground biomass.

### Gaps
- The IPCC 2019 Refinement did not update coastal wetlands; this was not verified here.
- India-specific Tier 2 values were not searched.

## 5. Plan Vivo, Gold Standard and ICVCM positions

### Takeaway
- **Plan Vivo:** verifies at least every 5 years. Its buffer was historically 20%; under PV Climate the buffer is risk-based. Mikoko Pamoja credits tree biomass only, with no soil.
- **Gold Standard:** has had a dedicated mangrove methodology since Aug 2024, with remote sensing allowed, and registered its first project in Feb 2026.
- **ICVCM:** no evidence was found that VM0033 is CCP-approved as of Oct 2026.

### Cited findings
- **Plan Vivo:**
  - Verifications take place "at least every 5 years" under PV Climate — [Plan Vivo risks page](https://www.planvivo.org/risks-of-non-delivery-and-reversals); [Plan Vivo ICAO presentation](https://www.icao.int:443/Meetings/carbonmarkets/Documents/02_Session5_Stephenson_Plan_Vivo.pdf)

## 6. Monitoring frequency vs verification frequency, and units

### Takeaway
No standard requires sub-annual stock measurement. Monitoring and verification periods are multi-year, with these known cadences:

| Requirement | Interval |
|---|---|
| VCS AFOLU registration | within 5 years of crediting start |
| Plan Vivo verification | at least every 5 years |
| VM0033 subsidence monitoring | at least once a year |
| VM0033 baseline and PDT reassessment | every 10 years |
| VCS VVB rotation | no more than 6 consecutive years verified by one VVB |

Quarterly satellite data fits the role of loss-event detection and permanence monitoring, which VCS v5.0 explicitly allows remote sensing to do, rather than issuance.

### Cited findings
- **VCS Standard v5.0** (16 Dec 2025) — [VCS Standard v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0)
  - AFOLU registration request is due within five years of the crediting period start (Table 7).
  - Failure to verify within the "prescribed period" puts a percentage of buffer credits on hold (§3.2.24; the period is defined in the Registration and Issuance Process).
  - Permanence must be monitored for at least 40 years (§3.2.25), and project longevity is at least 40 years (§3.2.12).
  - WRC crediting periods are 20–100 years.
  - A VVB may not verify more than six consecutive years (§4.1.27).
- **VM0033 monitoring:**
  - most parameters are monitored "at each monitoring period";
  - soil subsidence at least once a year;
  - PDT re-determined and baseline reassessed every 10 years.

  — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf)
- **IPCC:** vegetation establishment or extent "can be reviewed on a five year period" — [IPCC Ch. 4 p.4.29](https://www.ipcc-nggip.iges.or.jp/public/wetlands/pdf/Wetlands_separate_files/WS_Chp4_Coastal_Wetlands.pdf)
- **Units:**
  - all methodologies compute in t C and convert with 44/12, for example "−1.46 t C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ × 44/12" (VM0033 Eq. 33) and "44/12 × A × dSOC" (BM FR05.001 Eq. 4);
  - CH4 and N2O are converted with GWP;
  - credits are 1 t CO2e.

  — [VM0033 v2.1 PDF](https://verra.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/VM0033-Methodology-for-Tidal-Wetland-and-Seagrass-Restoration-v2.1.pdf); [BEE BM FR05.001](https://beeindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/BM%20FR05.001.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Recommended design:** quarterly Sentinel-1/2 observations used for:
  - loss and reversal detection;
  - canopy-cover tracking, which gates the soil default rate as in VM0033;
  - annual aggregation.

  Credits would be issued only on annual or multi-year verification events. Each event compares the cumulative adjusted net removals with the cumulative amount already issued (the VM0033 Eq. 93 logic).
- **Unit discipline:**
  - store stocks in t C/ha and rates in t C/ha/yr;
  - convert with × 44/12 to t CO2e;
  - multiply by area in ha;
  - apply the uncertainty adjustment to the cumulative net amount;
  - then subtract the buffer from the stock-change portion;
  - then subtract the credits already issued.

### Gaps
- The exact "prescribed period" for AFOLU verification in the VCS Registration and Issuance Process, and the percentage of buffer credits put on hold, were not retrieved. Historically this was 5 years, with buffer hold rules escalating after that; that history is unverified here.
- No standard was found that uses sub-annual monitoring for crediting.
